## What the served model is

A complementary log-log survival model. The chance that a country reports its first case within the horizon is `1 - exp(-exp(eta) * horizon)`, with `eta` linear in the features below. The form is not a choice of convenience: the question is when a first event happens, the quantity on the inside is a rate, and a rate times a horizon is a count. A logistic model fitted to the same labels would have no horizon in it at all, and its coefficients would silently mean something different for a 7-day question than for a 28-day one.

## Overfitting and leakage

- Parameters: 4 against 976 positives. Roughly 240 positives per parameter.
- Permuted-label held-out AUC: 0.535. A model that still ranked well after its training labels were shuffled would be reading a leak rather than a signal.
- The monotone constraint on the two travel terms is dropped for the permutation fit only. Constrained, a fit on noise can return only a zero or a positive slope, and a positive slope ranks the right way round, so the constraint alone would lift the permuted score above 0.5.
- Leave-one-outbreak-out train and test differ in base rate by about threefold, so the train-minus-test Brier column for those folds measures that difference and not memorisation. Read the forward-chained folds for the overfitting question: there train and test are the same outbreak.
- The two leakage guards assert on every fold that no row's inputs postdate its forecast date and that no outbreak appears on both sides of a split. They raise rather than warn.

## What the model does not do

- It does not order arrivals in time. Spearman correlation between the predicted rank and the actual day of first report is 0.03 across the held-out folds, which is no better than chance. The app's arrival-order view is a record of what happened, not a forecast of what will.
- It does not give a trustworthy absolute probability for a pathogen outside the panel. Both held-out calibration intercepts are about one logit from zero, in opposite directions, which is the threefold base-rate difference between COVID-19 and mpox. The served band comes from refitting without each outbreak, so it is the spread between those two worlds rather than a confidence interval.
- It says nothing where no passengers arrive from an affected country. 7.0% of panel rows are in that position; serving reports them as unavailable rather than returning the intercept as if it were a finding.

## Calibration: what was tried and rejected

The held-out slopes are 0.92 and 1.21 - the predictions move with the observed rates about one for one. Only the level is wrong, and it is wrong in opposite directions on the two pathogens. So the obvious repair is to keep the transferred slopes and refit the intercept online, on the live outbreak's own first reports, using only weeks whose 28-day label window has already closed. That was implemented and measured.

| held-out outbreak | | AUC | Brier | calibration slope | calibration intercept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| covid_2020 | transferred intercept | 0.783 | 0.250 | 0.92 | 1.11 |
| covid_2020 | refitted online | 0.569 | 0.261 | 0.29 | -0.18 |
| mpox_2022 | transferred intercept | 0.768 | 0.149 | 1.21 | -1.64 |
| mpox_2022 | refitted online | 0.670 | 0.116 | 0.33 | -1.94 |

It does what it was meant to do to COVID-19's level, moving the intercept from 1.11 to -0.18, inside the gate. It costs the ordering: AUC falls from 0.783 to 0.569 on COVID-19 and from 0.768 to 0.670 on mpox, and the slope collapses on both. The reason is structural rather than a tuning failure. An intercept refitted each week is a different intercept each week, so rows from different weeks are no longer scored on one scale, and the ordering that the gates do pass is exactly what gets destroyed. It would buy a calibration gate by spending the only thing the model has earned.

That is why the served status is `ranking_validated` and not `calibrated`, and why nothing in the serving path rescales the probability. The level of a first-report hazard is a property of the pathogen and of how hard countries are looking for it; two outbreaks are not enough to learn it, and no amount of post-processing on two outbreaks will add a third.

## The prevalence term

`log_lambda` and `log_inbound` are 0.96-correlated across the panel, and the fitted coefficient on `log_lambda` sits at its constrained floor of zero. On this evidence the prevalence estimate - reported cases, times an infectious period, times an under-reporting multiplier, divided by origin population - adds nothing to raw passenger volume, and on its own it ranks worse (see `hazard_lambda_only` against `hazard_inbound_only` above).

That is a finding about the inputs, not a reason to drop the term. The panel's case counts come from two outbreaks whose reporting was dominated by a handful of large origins, so there is little independent variation in prevalence to fit. The term stays, constrained to be non-negative, so that its fitted zero is a measurement that this report can show rather than an omission nobody can see.
